Correct, effective sentences

A correct sentence needs at least one independent clause: it has a subject and a finite verb, and it expresses one complete idea. This lesson helps you learn the 4 sentence types, join sentences with the right punctuation and avoid the 5 sentence errors that are common with Vietnamese learners.

1. Understand clauses

Independent clause

Has a subject + a finite verb and can stand alone.

Online learning is flexible.

Dependent clause

Starts with a subordinating word (because, although, when, which…) and cannot stand alone.

because students can study at any time

A clause needs a subject and a finite verb. The rapid growth of online shopping is a noun phrase with no verb; the other two both contain is growing.
Model: The rapid growth of online shopping has hurt small shops.
3Can “when students finish their final exams” stand alone as a sentence?
It has a subject (students) and a verb (finish), but when makes it dependent, so it must join an independent clause.
Model: When students finish their final exams, many of them look for summer jobs.
4How many independent clauses are there in “Although tuition is free, many families cannot afford textbooks.”?
Only many families cannot afford textbooks can stand alone. Although tuition is free has a subject and a verb, but it is a dependent clause.
Model: Although tuition is free, many families cannot afford textbooks.
5Choose the verb that completes this S + V sentence: Since 2020, online courses .
S + V needs a verb that is complete on its own. Attract needs an object (attracted what?), and become needs a complement (became what?).
Model: Since 2020, online courses have multiplied.
6Which pattern does “Working from home has become common.” follow?
Common describes the subject working from home after the linking verb become, so it is a complement, not an object.
Model: Working from home has become common.
7Which pattern does “Remote workers need reliable internet.” follow?
Reliable internet answers need what? and is a different thing from the workers, so it is an object. A complement describes the subject.
Model: Remote workers need reliable internet.

Offer takes two objects: a person (its staff) and a thing (a pay rise). A pay rise does not describe the staff, so it is not a complement.
Model: The company offered its staff a pay rise.

Awake describes the object some teenagers, so it is a complement. The other two sentences are S + V + O + O and S + V + C.
Model: Online games keep some teenagers awake at night.

Ms Hoa and team leader are the same person, so team leader completes the object: S + V + O + C. Two objects are two different things.

2. Four sentence types

Writing at band 6.5+ or VSTEP level 4 needs a mix of sentence types. Only simple sentences sound choppy; only long sentences invite mistakes. An easy balance to control: about half simple or compound sentences, half complex sentences.

Simple

Simple sentence

1 independent clause

Many young people now work from home.

Compound

Compound sentence

Independent clause, FANBOYSIndependent clause

Remote work saves time, but it can be lonely.

Complex

Complex sentence

Independent clause+Dependent clause

Although remote work saves time, it can be lonely.

Compound-complex

Compound-complex sentence

2 independent clauses+≥1 dependent clause

Although remote work saves time, it can be lonely, so many firms now offer hybrid schedules.

Four sentence types

0/10
1What type of sentence is “Many students attend lectures in the morning and work in cafés at night.”?
There is one subject (many students) with two verbs joined by and. A compound sentence needs two clauses, each with its own subject.
Model: Many students attend lectures in the morning and work in cafés at night.
2What type of sentence is “The salary was low, yet she accepted the job.”?
Yet is a FANBOYS word: it joins two independent clauses, with a comma before it. Neither clause is dependent.
Model: The salary was low, yet she accepted the job.
3What type of sentence is “Parents worry when their children spend too much time online.”?
When their children spend too much time online is a dependent clause, so the sentence is complex. In the middle of the sentence it needs no comma.
Model: Parents worry when their children spend too much time online.
4What type of sentence is “Employees who feel valued rarely leave, and they recommend the firm to friends.”?
Two independent clauses are joined by , and, and who feel valued is a relative (dependent) clause. Two independent clauses + one dependent clause = compound-complex.
Model: Employees who feel valued rarely leave, and they recommend the firm to friends.
5Make it a complex sentence: the course was online, many older learners found it difficult.
Because makes the first clause dependent, which gives a complex sentence. With So or However, you get two independent clauses joined only by a comma.
Model: Because the course was online, many older learners found it difficult.
6Which sentence is compound?
Only this one joins two independent clauses with a FANBOYS word. Although and that begin dependent clauses, so the other two are complex.
Model: Some firms pay well, but others offer more holidays.
7Which sentence is compound-complex?
It has two independent clauses joined by , so and one dependent clause (If the internet goes down). The other two are complex and compound.
Model: If the internet goes down, staff cannot work, so they go home.
8A paragraph uses only simple sentences. What is the main problem?
Only simple sentences sound choppy and repetitive. Using only long sentences causes the opposite problem: more grammar mistakes.
9A paragraph has five long compound-complex sentences. What is the best change?
Only long sentences invite grammar mistakes. Aim for a mix: about half simple or compound sentences and half complex ones.
10What type of sentence is “Many people believe that robots will take their jobs.”?
That robots will take their jobs is a noun clause, a kind of dependent clause, used as the object. One independent + one dependent clause = complex.
Model: Many people believe that robots will take their jobs.

3. Three ways to join sentences, and the punctuation for each

Type of connectorCommon wordsPunctuationExample
Coordinating conjunctions (FANBOYS)for, and, nor, but, or, yet, soa comma before the connectorFees are high, so fewer students apply.
Subordinating conjunctionsbecause, although, while, when, if, unless, since, whereasat the start: a comma after the dependent clause; in the middle: usually no commaBecause fees are high, fewer students apply. / Fewer students apply because fees are high.
Linking adverbshowever, therefore, moreover, as a result, in addition, for examplea semicolon before and a comma after; or start a new sentenceFees are high; therefore, fewer students apply. / Fees are high. As a result, fewer students apply.
Note
However, therefore, moreover cannot join two clauses with a comma. Writing “Fees are high, however many students apply” is a comma splice error.
Practice

Joining sentences and punctuation

0/10
1Many graduates want to work abroad few can afford the visa costs.
But is a FANBOYS word: put a comma before it and nothing after it. However cannot join two clauses with only a comma.
Model: Many graduates want to work abroad, but few can afford the visa costs.
2Although the job pays well the hours are long.
After a dependent clause at the start, use a comma. Do not add but: although already shows the contrast. A semicolon cannot follow a dependent clause.
Model: Although the job pays well, the hours are long.
3Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
A because clause after the main clause needs no comma and no full stop. Cut off by a full stop, it becomes a fragment.
Model: Fewer people buy newspapers because news is free online.
4Online courses are cheap many learners never finish them.
However is a linking adverb, not a conjunction: a semicolon before it, a comma after it. With a comma before it, the sentence is a comma splice.
Model: Online courses are cheap; however, many learners never finish them.
5Which sentence uses as a result correctly?
As a result is a linking adverb. Start a new sentence (or use a semicolon) before it; a comma alone between the two clauses is a comma splice.
Model: Rents rose. As a result, many couples moved in with their parents.
6What is wrong with “The library stays open until midnight, however it is often empty.”?
However cannot join two clauses after a comma. Write …until midnight; however, it is often empty, or start a new sentence with However,.
Model: The library stays open until midnight; however, it is often empty.
7Many parents have full-time jobs; , grandparents often look after the children.
A semicolon before and a comma after signal a linking adverb such as as a result. So and because are conjunctions and never take this punctuation.
Model: Many parents have full-time jobs; as a result, grandparents often look after the children.
8Young people leave their villages there are more jobs in cities.
With no punctuation in the middle, you need a subordinating conjunction such as because. However and therefore are linking adverbs and need a semicolon or a full stop.
Model: Young people leave their villages because there are more jobs in cities.
9Join the two sentences correctly: Broadband is fast in cities. It is slow in rural areas.
Put the comma before the FANBOYS word. A comma alone makes a comma splice, and a comma after but is in the wrong place.
Model: Broadband is fast in cities, but it is slow in rural areas.
10Which group of words joins two clauses with a comma before and nothing after?
FANBOYS words take a comma before them. Linking adverbs need a semicolon and a comma, and subordinators in the middle of a sentence usually take no comma.

These are the 10 sentence patterns used in Mr Ha's Academic Writing course. Practise each pattern on the same topic until you know it well, then use it in your writing: each paragraph should have at least 3 different patterns.

5. Five common sentence errors and how to fix them

Wrong
Correct
1. Fragment
Because the city is too crowded.A dependent clause standing alone.
Many families move out because the city is too crowded.
2. Run-on
Technology changes fast people must keep learning.Two independent clauses with no connector and no punctuation.
Technology changes fast, so people must keep learning.
3. Comma splice
Exercise is important, it reduces stress.A comma is not strong enough to join two independent clauses.
Exercise is important because it reduces stress. / Exercise is important; it reduces stress.
4. Subject–verb disagreement
The number of tourists have increased.The main subject is the number (singular).
The number of tourists has increased.
5. Dangling modifier
Living in the countryside, the air is fresh.“Living…” must refer to people, but the subject is “the air”.
Living in the countryside, people enjoy fresh air.

Three more “weak sentence” problems

Faulty parallelism

The course is cheap, flexible and it helps you a lot.

→ The course is cheap, flexible and useful. (parts joined by and/or/not only…but also must have the same form)

Choppy sentences

Cars are cheap. Cars are convenient. Cars pollute the air.

→ Although cars are cheap and convenient, they pollute the air.

Stringy, wordy sentences

In my opinion, I think that it is a fact that the reason why… is because…

→ I believe … because … (cut the extra words, one idea per sentence)

0/10

Since makes the clause dependent, so it cannot stand alone. Add an independent clause, e.g. …, older customers need more help.
Model: Since most banks have moved their services online, older customers need more help.
2Which sentence is a run-on?
A run-on puts two independent clauses together with no connector and no punctuation. The others use , and or one subject with two verbs.
Model: Smart watches track our health, and they also collect data.
3Choose the best fix for “My sister graduated in May, she still lives at home.”
, but joins two independent clauses correctly. A comma before however is still a comma splice, and although… but uses two connectors for one link.
Model: My sister graduated in May, but she still lives at home.
4One of the main reasons for youth unemployment a lack of skills.
The main subject is One (singular), so the verb is is. Reasons sits inside the phrase, and being is not a finite verb.
Model: One of the main reasons for youth unemployment is a lack of skills.
5Children who play on a tablet for hours less time outdoors.
The subject is Children (plural). A tablet is inside the relative clause, so it does not control the main verb.
Model: Children who play on a tablet for hours spend less time outdoors.
6Which version of “Working from home, my productivity has improved.” is correct?
The phrase Working from home must describe the subject after the comma. A person can work from home; productivity cannot.

7A good manager listens to staff, gives clear feedback and .
Items joined by and need the same form: listens…, gives… and rewards… are three verbs in the present simple.

9Which is the best version of “In my opinion, I personally think that the reason why young people change jobs is because they want more pay.”?
Cut repeated meaning: in my opinion and I think say the same thing, and the reason why… is because says because twice.

10What is wrong with “The job requires patience, creativity and being organised.”?
Patience and creativity are nouns, but being organised has a different form. Write patience, creativity and good organisation.
Model: The job requires patience, creativity and good organisation.

Practice

Varying your sentences

0/10
1Combine with a relative clause: Some employees take regular breaks. They make fewer mistakes.
Who replaces they, so do not repeat the subject. Use who, not which, for people.
Model: Employees who take regular breaks make fewer mistakes.
2Parents children use social media should check the privacy settings.
Whose shows possession: the children belong to the parents. Who would have to be the subject of use, but children is already the subject.
Model: Parents whose children use social media should check the privacy settings.
3, the online course now has 10,000 learners.
Someone launched the course (passive meaning), so use the V3 form Launched. Launching would mean the course launched something itself.
Model: Launched in 2021, the online course now has 10,000 learners.
4Combine with a participle phrase: Many graduates want work experience. They accept unpaid internships.
Graduates do the wanting (active), so use Wanting. The subject after the comma must be the people who want, many graduates, or the modifier dangles.
Model: Wanting work experience, many graduates accept unpaid internships.
5 is a growing concern for teachers.
A clause used as a subject needs that (or the fact that) in front. A bare clause cannot be a subject, and what does not fit a clause that is already complete.
Model: That students copy answers from AI is a growing concern for teachers.
6Combine with a noun clause: How do children use the internet? Parents should know this.
Inside a noun clause, use statement word order (how their children use), not question order (how do…). The whole sentence is a statement, so it ends with a full stop.
Model: Parents should know how their children use the internet.
7Rewrite in the passive: “The university awards scholarships to the top 5% of students.”
The passive is be + V3: are awarded. Are awarding is active, and without are there is no finite verb, so the last option is a fragment.
Model: Scholarships are awarded to the top 5% of students.
8Since 2020, thousands of office jobs by software.
By software names the doer, so the jobs receive the action: passive have been replaced. Active have replaced would need an object.
Model: Since 2020, thousands of office jobs have been replaced by software.
9Combine with a conditional: Students should revise early. Otherwise, they will panic before exams.
First conditional: If + present simple, then will + verb. Unless already means if not, so unless… do not is a double negative.
Model: If students do not revise early, they will panic before exams.
10Rewrite with It is + adjective + that: Children must learn to use the internet safely.
The frame is It is + adjective + that + subject + verb. This cannot replace the empty subject It, and the verb after children takes no to.
Model: It is essential that children learn to use the internet safely.

Writing an academic paragraph

The paragraph is the “building block” of every piece of writing: each IELTS body paragraph and each point in a VSTEP email is a paragraph. A good paragraph deals with one main idea, states it in the first sentence, then proves it with explanation and examples.

120–200 words3 parts5 writing steps

1. The 3-part structure of a paragraph

Part 1

Topic sentence

The first sentence of the paragraph. It has a topic + a controlling idea, that is, what you will prove about the topic.

Part 2

Supporting sentences

2–3 main supporting points; each point has minor details: explanation, examples, figures, results. This is the longest part.

Part 3

Concluding sentence

Restate the topic sentence in different words, or sum up the points, or give a final comment.

TS (1 sentence)→Supporting point 1 (1 sentence) + details/examples (2–3 sentences)→Supporting point 2 (1 sentence) + details/examples (2–3 sentences)→CS (1 sentence)

About 8–10 sentences in total. Think of a sandwich: the topic sentence and the concluding sentence are the two slices of bread, and the support is the filling.

Six features of an effective paragraph

1

Clear organisation

All 3 parts, in the right order.

2

Full development

Each supporting point has details and examples.

3

Unity

Only discuss the idea in the topic sentence.

4

Coherence and cohesion

Ideas in logical order, with linking words.

5

Vocabulary

Varied, with synonyms and correct collocations.

6

Sentences

Varied sentence patterns; no fragments, run-ons, comma splices, choppy sentences or rambling sentences.

3. Make an outline: main points and minor details

Before you write, find ideas in one of three ways: listing (note down every idea quickly), a mind map (topic in the middle, branches for supporting points, smaller branches for details), then turn it into a bullet-point outline. Each main point answers the question “why / how?” for the topic sentence; each minor detail makes the main point clearer with an explanation, a description, figures or an example. Ask who, what, when, where, why, how to find details.

Sample outline

TS: Studying abroad helps young people become more independent.

  • Main point 1: manage daily life alone
    • explanation: cook, pay bills, deal with problems without parents
    • example: a student in Australia learns to budget a monthly allowance
  • Main point 2: make decisions about study and career
    • explanation: choose courses, look for part-time jobs
    • result: more confident when they start work

Tip
The PEEL model is used for IELTS body paragraphs: Point (main point) → Explain (explanation) → Example (example) → Link (link back to the question). It is the same main point and minor detail outline above, written as a short routine.

6. Check unity and coherence

Unity

  • The whole paragraph discusses only one main idea.
  • Every sentence supports the controlling idea. Delete any sentence that goes off topic.

Coherence

  • Repeat key nouns or use synonyms.
  • Keep pronouns consistent (students → they, do not switch to you).
  • Use linking words with the right function.
  • Put ideas in a logical order: time, importance, cause → effect.

9. Reading into writing: summarise an article as a paragraph

This is the core homework task in the Academic Writing course: read an academic article (for example on climate change or hunger), then rewrite its main ideas as a well-structured paragraph. You learn topic vocabulary and practise paragraph structure at the same time.

  1. Skim to get the general idea of the article.
  2. Read closely (scan) and underline key words, good phrases, collocations and sentence structures worth learning. Write them in your vocabulary notebook.
  3. Decide the topic and controlling idea of the paragraph you will write (for example: the main causes of climate change).
  4. Draw a mind map, then write: TS → supporting point 1 + 2 details → supporting point 2 + 2 details → CS.
  5. Check unity, coherence and cohesion yourself before you hand it in.
Step up
At first you may reuse the wording of the article (you are only practising structure). Once you are confident, move on to paraphrasing in your own words.
